The big idea

Light under a closed door

The mask is the door: shut, controlled, polite. The emotion is the lit room behind it. For a split second, the gap at the bottom gives it away. That gap is a micro-expression: a complete, involuntary expression lasting roughly 1/25 to 1/2 of a second.

Macro0.5–4 s

The everyday expression. Whole face, easy to see, often deliberate. What you read in conversation without trying.

Micro<0.5 s

Full expression, compressed in time. Appears when a feeling is being concealed, or is so new the person hasn't registered it yet.

SubtleLow-intensity

Any duration, but faint or in one region only: a brow twitch, a lip press. Often the first ripple of an emotion, or one being held down.

The machinery · FACS

Keys and chords

The Facial Action Coding System (Ekman & Friesen, 1978) numbers every visible muscle movement as an Action Unit, or AU. Think of AUs as piano keys and emotions as chords. You don't memorise a picture of "sad"; you learn that sadness is the chord 1 + 4 + 15, and then you can hear it in any face. Press a chord.

AU 1 + 4 + 15

Where to look

Three zones, one story

Brow

Forehead and eyebrows. The hardest zone to control on purpose, so it leaks the most. Sadness and fear live here.

Eyes

Lids and cheeks. Width tells arousal (surprise, fear); tightness tells intent (anger); crinkles tell real joy.

Mouth

Lips, chin and jaw. The most watched zone, so it is the most managed. People smile over everything else.

Read the face like a three-panel comic.
One panel can lie. When the brow, eyes and mouth tell different stories, the brow is usually the honest one.

Use it well

A smoke alarm, not a fire report

A micro-expression tells you that an emotion fired. It doesn't tell you why, or what the person will do about it.

Which, not why

An innocent person afraid of being disbelieved shows the same fear as a guilty one afraid of being caught. Ekman called misreading this the Othello error.

Not a lie detector

Across hundreds of studies, people spot lies at about 54%, barely above a coin flip. Faces flag hot spots worth asking about; they don't deliver verdicts.

Context matters

How universal the basic expressions are is still debated, and culture shapes when people show them. Treat every read as a hypothesis, then check it against words, voice and situation.

Spot the Fake · 6 rounds

Felt or performed?

Some muscles are hard to move on command. Ekman called them reliable muscles: when they're missing, the expression is probably posed. Timing gives fakes away too. Felt expressions rise like a dimmer; posed ones snap on like a switch.
