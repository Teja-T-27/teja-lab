The eight functions come in four opposed pairs. Every type's top four functions are exactly two complete pairs: one for taking in information, one for making decisions. Choose one of each and you get a family of four types that share the same toolkit and differ only in which tool they reach for first.

The Myers-Briggs framework is a useful vocabulary for talking about how people differ. As a measuring instrument it has well-documented problems, and the cognitive-function layer used on this site has even less research behind it.

Reasonable uses

A shared language for differences in energy, attention and decision style. Team conversations. Self-reflection, held loosely.

Poor uses

Hiring and promotion. Diagnosing anyone. Predicting whether two people will get along. The Pairings tab is type lore, not evidence.
